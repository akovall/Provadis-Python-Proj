CHANGES
=======

Result on mushroom.jpg: 726 -> 244 paths, 862 KB -> 247 KB, smooth edges instead of jagged ones.

Smoother vectors (preprocess.py, vectorize.py)
- Stronger noise removal (mean-shift filter) before reducing colors.
- Colors are picked in Lab color space, and similar colors are merged by color distance (ΔE).
- Image is upscaled 2x and cleaned (each pixel takes the most common color around it),
  which removes stair-steps and specks.
- vtracer no longer reduces colors a second time, it only traces shapes.
- One "detail" value (1-10) controls vtracer's curve settings (corners, segment length).
- A background rectangle is painted behind the SVG so the image corners are not rounded off.

Web page (app.py, static/index.html)
- Start: uvicorn app:app --reload, then open http://127.0.0.1:8000
- Drag and drop an image, drag across the preview to compare original and SVG.
- Sliders: Curve detail, Color merge range, Max colors, Remove specks.
- Shows colors, paths, curves and file size, plus a Download SVG button.

Command line (main.py)
- New options: --detail, --merge, --colors, --speckle
  Example: python main.py photo.jpg --detail 2 --merge 15

Other
- requirements.txt: added fastapi, uvicorn, python-multipart.
- README.md: updated.


Smoother curves, fewer points (curves.py, round 2)
- vtracer made many tiny Bezier segments (some ~2 px long), even on round edges.
- New step refits every outline: finds real corners, smooths the line between them,
  then fits as few Bezier curves as possible within an error tolerance.
- Curve detail slider now controls that tolerance (6 px at 1 down to 0.6 px at 10).
  Mushroom: ~8,400 curves from vtracer -> 940 at detail 1, 1,130 at detail 4, 4,800 at detail 10.
- Web page: moving only the detail slider skips tracing and just refits (~1.5-4 s).


Transparent background (preprocess.py, round 3)
- Before: the background was just another color, so white parts inside an object
  (like the white marks on the panda's hands) were treated the same as the background.
- Now the object is separated from the background first (an alpha mask):
  - a real alpha channel is used if the image has one,
  - otherwise the background is the area of border-like colors that touches the image edge.
    Same-colored areas inside the object are not connected to the edge, so they stay real shapes.
- Also removes painted checkerboard "fake transparency" backgrounds.
- The SVG has a transparent background. Web page: "Remove background" checkbox (on by default),
  preview shows a checkerboard. CLI: --keep-background to turn it off.


Sharp details and match with the original (round 4)
- Sharp parts of an outline (tips, claws, small shapes) now get more Bezier points and less
  smoothing; gentle curves still get few points.
- Small images are enlarged to ~1024 px before processing, so thin lines (the white marks on
  the panda's hands, its claws) are no longer smoothed away. The SVG keeps the original size.
- Soft anti-aliased edges are sharpened (toggle filter) before choosing colors, so they do not
  become extra bands of in-between colors. Faint lines (the mushroom cap lines) are kept.
- Curve refitting runs on all CPU cores.
- Tested by rendering each SVG and comparing it pixel by pixel with the original
  (average color difference ΔE, lower is closer):
    panda1.png   before 1.43 -> now about 1.2 (hand marks and claws kept)
    mushroom.jpg before 6.6  -> now about 0.95 (with background kept)

Presets and Recommended (quality.py, app.py, index.html)
- Preset buttons: Logo / flat, Illustration, Detailed, Extra smooth.
- "Recommended for this image": tries several settings, draws each SVG back into pixels,
  compares it with the original and picks the simplest one that still matches closely.
  Results: panda -> 8 colors, detail 2, match 1.29; mushroom -> 9 colors, detail 2, match 1.13.
- New "Match with original" value under the stats (ΔE, under ~2 is hard to see).


Smooth outlines (strokes.py, round 5)
- Removed the four fixed presets (Logo, Illustration, Detailed, Extra smooth).
  "Recommended for this image" and the Match value stay.
- Outlines are found automatically: dark colors (Lab lightness < 50) whose shapes are long and
  thin (at most 1.5% of the image size wide). They are reduced to a smooth centerline, and the
  thickness is measured from the original at every point along it (smoothed, so it changes
  gradually). Each line is drawn as a filled ribbon around the centerline, so a line that is
  thicker in one part of the picture and thinner in another stays that way.
  Thick parts attached to a line (the panda's nose on its mouth) stay filled shapes.
  The pixels under the stroke go to the neighboring colors, so no wobbly edge shows.
- Results: mushroom outline is smooth and thicker/thinner in the same places as the original
  (match 0.94-1.01, about the same as without outlines); panda's mouth is one clean line that
  thins toward its ends.
- Web page: "Smooth outlines" checkbox (on by default). CLI: --no-outlines to turn it off.
- Tried: pushing all anti-aliased pixels to the darker color. It made every dark area grow
  (panda match 1.15 -> 1.83), so it was removed; the strokes measure the outline width instead.


Simple shapes (shapes.py, round 6)
- Areas of one color that are almost exactly an ellipse, circle or rectangle (at least 95%
  overlap with the fitted shape, no holes) are drawn as real SVG <ellipse>, <circle> or <rect>
  instead of traced paths: perfectly smooth, no Bezier points.
- Second pass: a shape partly hidden behind one found before (a cheek behind an eye) is fitted
  only on its visible edge and drawn as a full shape underneath.
- Mushroom: both eyes and both cheeks become ellipses (match 1.12 -> 1.06).
  Panda: nothing qualifies (its pupils and eye patches are not clean ellipses), so it is unchanged.
- Web page: "Detect simple shapes" checkbox (on by default); the "Paths" stat is now "Shapes".
  CLI: --no-shapes to turn it off.
- Outlines now keep their original thickness (thicker and thinner parts), see round 5.


Stacked layers instead of cut-out pieces (layers.py, round 7)
- vtracer is gone. The image is split into layers, one per connected area of one color,
  sorted from the biggest (bottom) to the smallest (top).
- Layers are no longer cut out where smaller layers lie on them: holes and notches under the
  layers on top are filled in. The face is one whole shape with the eyes on top; the panda's
  body is one shape with the dark patches lying on it.
- Each layer is first checked against simple forms (ellipse, circle, rectangle; only the visible
  part has to match). All other layers get smooth Bezier curves with sharp corners kept where
  the edge really turns.
- Big to small is the rule, with one exception: a layer that becomes a simple form when a
  neighboring form may lie on top of it moves just below that form. So the mushroom's cheeks are
  ellipses lying under the eyes (as in the picture), although they are smaller than the eyes.
- Outlines are drawn last, over everything, and the layers below extend under them.
- Before layering, thin anti-aliasing fringes between two big areas are split between them,
  and specks join the most similar neighbor color (so faint lines stay continuous).
- Every layer reaches 4 px under the layers on top of it, so no gaps open between them.
- Edge sharpening is lighter now (it made the panda's nose and pupils jagged).
- Results: panda 52 layers (body, eye patches, cheeks and inner ears are ellipses), match 1.34;
  mushroom 133 layers (background rectangle, eyes and cheeks ellipses), match 1.03;
  pixel-art test.png unchanged by the forms (1.48 with, 1.47 without).
  Converting is faster than with vtracer, and the detail slider takes about 1 s.


THINGS TO KNOW
==============

- Speed: a slider change takes about 2-8 s (detail only: ~2 s), "Recommended" about 13 s.
  Uploading takes a bit longer than before because of the extra edge sharpening.
- At very low detail, thin lines (like the dark outline) can look a little thinner in places,
  because every color shape is smoothed on its own.
- Background removal works from the border, so if the object touches the image edge with a
  color close to the background, that part can be removed too. Use --keep-background then.
- Recommended does not change "Remove background", "Remove specks", "Smooth outlines" or
  "Detect simple shapes"; set those first.
- Shapes with holes are skipped (anything inside the hole would be hidden under the shape).
- The panda's nose and pupils still have small bumps; they come from its soft, blurry edges.
- Exact ellipses are a little different from soft hand-drawn ovals, so the panda's match is a
  bit higher with forms (1.34) than without (1.16). Untick "Detect simple shapes" if that matters.
- Recommended is only on the web page, not in the command line.
- Where two lines cross or meet, the thickness is capped at 2x the line's usual width so the
  junction does not swell.
- Very thin tapered line tips (under 4 px) stay filled shapes and can look a bit lighter.
- Finding outlines adds about 2-4 s when the colors or specks change (not for the detail slider).
- The web page was tested through its API, not clicked through in a browser yet.
- Nothing is committed yet.
